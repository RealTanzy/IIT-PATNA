1 Introduction034
Chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting (Wei et al.,035
2022) has become the standard way to elicit step-036
by-step reasoning from large language models037
(LLMs). By generating intermediate reasoning038
steps before a final answer, CoT often improves per-039
formance on arithmetic, commonsense, and sym-040
bolic tasks. However, its core generation process041
is still strictlylinear: the model commits to a sin-042
gle trace s1 →s 2 → · · · →s T , and later steps043
are conditioned on all earlier commitments. This 044
design creates three well-known pressures. First, 045
an early mistake can propagate through the entire 046
trace because the model has no built-in backtrack- 047
ing mechanism. Second, questions that require 048
integrating several independent pieces of evidence 049
can be awkward to solve with a single left-to-right 050
narrative. Third, CoT has no explicit search objec- 051
tive that trades off trace length, uncertainty, and 052
alternative hypotheses.053
These limitations motivate search-based reason- 054
ing. If the model can explore multiple partial traces, 055
then it can reconsider early commitments, compare 056
candidate branches, and prioritize promising rea- 057
soning states. In this paper, we study a budgeted 058
A*-style search procedure over natural-language 059
reasoning traces. The language model generates 060
successor steps, a step cost penalizes both trace 061
length and low-confidence steps, and a conserva- 062
tive heuristic prioritizes which partial trace to ex- 063
pand next. We pair this search procedure with 064
standard CoT and ask a deliberately narrower ques- 065
tion than “does search replace CoT?” Our focus 066
is whether search and linear reasoning arecomple- 067
mentary, and whether simple routing mechanisms 068
can exploit that complementarity.069
Motivating example.On StrategyQA, linear 070
CoT can pursue a locally plausible but ultimately 071
irrelevant chain, while A* can surface the deci- 072
sive evidence earlier by exploring alternative par- 073
tial traces. For example, on the question “Is shrimp 074
scampi definitely free of plastic?”, CoT misses the 075
key fact of microplastic accumulation in seafood, 076
whereas A* reaches that premise early and answers 077
correctly. This illustrates the central hypothesis of078
the paper: CoT and search are complementary, and 079
the real challenge is deciding when to use which.080

benchmarks, CoT and A* solve different subsets 082
of examples. On StrategyQA, A* outperforms 083

2.5 Routing Strategies252
Given the observed complementarity between CoT253
and A*, we define routing functions R:Q →254
{CoT, A∗} that select the strategy on a per-instance255
basis.256
Semantic entropy.We compute the entropy257
of the answer distribution obtained from self-258
consistency sampling under CoT. High-entropy in-259
stances are routed to A*, while low-entropy in-260
stances keep the CoT answer.261
Feature-based random forest.We extract hand- 262
crafted features from the problem text, includ- 263
ing question length, logical connectives, negation 264
markers, and question-type indicators, and train a 265
random forest classifier to predict which reasoning 266
strategy will succeed.267
LLM-as-critic.When CoT and A* disagree, we 268
invoke a stronger critic model Mcritic (DeepSeek- 269
R1-Distill-Qwen-14B). The critic receives both 270

3.2 Qualitative Analysis345
To understandwhyCoT and A* differ, we manually 346
inspected representative disagreement cases across 347
StrategyQA, LogicQA, and HotpotQA. Rather than 348
treating qualitative analysis as a collection of iso- 349
lated examples, we organize these cases into recur- 350
ringreasoning regimes. The resulting picture is 351
consistent with the quantitative results: A* is not 352
uniformly better than CoT, but helps in problem 353
settings where reasoning requires exploration over 354
multiple plausible partial trajectories, while CoT re- 355
mains competitive or preferable when the solution 356
path is naturally linear and can be executed reliably 357
in a single forward pass. Detailed examples for 358
each category are provided in Appendix A.359
When A* helps.Across all three datasets, A* 360
is most useful when the reasoning problem has 361
abranching structure: multiple partial hypothe- 362
ses must be considered, compared, or eliminated 363
before the correct conclusion becomes clear. We 364

observe three especially common patterns.365
First, A* helps innon-linear evidence aggre-366
gation, where the decisive evidence is distributed367
across several facts that do not naturally compose368
into a single fluent chain. In such cases, CoT often369
commits early to one locally plausible narrative and370
then continues refining that narrative even when it371
is globally suboptimal. Search mitigates this by372
allowing alternative branches to remain alive long373
enough for the more informative path to surface.374
Second, A* is advantageous inhypothesis elimi-375
nationproblems, especially in multiple-choice log-376
ical reasoning. Here the difficulty is often not de-377

plausible options that match the wording while fail-379

across alternatives and compare them more explic-381

Third, A* is helpful inmulti-hop composition,385

the answer depends on traversing intermediate en-387

less like a source of extra knowledge and more like389
a mechanism for preserving multiple partial chains390
until the relevant bridge entity is found.391
Why these gains arise.The common thread in392
the above cases is that A* provides a way to defer393
commitment. CoT generates a single left-to-right394
trajectory, which is efficient when that trajectory is395
correct, but brittle when early steps are only par-396
tially informative or mildly misleading. In contrast,397
A* imposes structure over the reasoning process: it398
treats intermediate thoughts as expandable states,399
and this makes it possible to revisit the search space400
when the first promising line of reasoning turns out401
not to be the best one. Qualitatively, the advantage402
is therefore not just “more steps,” butbetter allo-403
cation of reasoning effort across competing partial404
solutions.405
When CoT remains preferable.The same anal-406
ysis also makes clear that search is not always ben-407
eficial. CoT often performs better when the task408
is effectivelysingle-path reasoning: the answer409
follows from direct factual recall, a short deter-410
ministic comparison, or a coherent argumentative411
trajectory that does not benefit from branching. In412
these cases, A* can degrade performance by intro-413
ducing unnecessary exploration. We observe two414
frequent regimes where this happens.415
The first isdirect factual reasoning, where the 416
problem reduces to retrieving a few stable facts 417
and combining them straightforwardly. CoT often 418
succeeds because a single fluent chain is enough, 419
whereas A* may overcomplicate the problem by 420
searching over semantically adjacent but irrelevant 421
distinctions.422
The second isglobally coherent argumentation, 423
especially in some LogicQA instances. These prob- 424
lems reward maintaining a stable abstract frame 425
over the entire reasoning process. CoT can pre- 426
serve such a frame naturally through a single expla- 427
nation, while A* may fragment the reasoning into 428
locally plausible steps that do not combine into the 429
strongest global argument.430
Characteristic failure modes of A*.Our man- 431
ual inspection also reveals that A* has systematic 432
failure modes rather than random errors. The most 433
prominent one isoverthinking simple problems: 434
when the answer is accessible via direct recall or 435
a short inference, search may create unnecessary 436
distinctions and drift away from the obvious solu- 437
tion. A second failure mode isspurious associative 438
expansion, where one branch of the search picks 439
up a weak semantic connection and the search pro- 440

third isbudget waste through repetition or near- 443
duplicate expansions, where search spends steps 444
rephrasing or revisiting the same intermediate fact 445
instead of exploring genuinely new possibilities.446
These failure modes are important because they 447
show that the cost of search is not merely computa- 448
tional. Search changes theshapeof reasoning, and 449
this can hurt when the original problem does not 450
require exploration in the first place.451

proportion of relevant steps, where relevant steps 507
remain on-topic and avoid introducing unnecessary 508
concepts.509
Relevance is less diagnostic. In StrategyQA, CoT 510
remains more relevant than A* when both are 511
wrong (2.82 vs. 1.55), while in LogicQA both meth- 512
ods stay moderately relevant even when incorrect 513
(2.76 for CoT, 2.53 for A*). Thus, staying on-topic 514
is not enough: a trace may remain relevant yet still 515
miss the decisive inferential steps. Overall, the 516
annotations indicate that the main difference be- 517
tween CoT and A* on disagreement cases lies in 518
which method better covers the decisive reasoning 519
steps, while relevance is only weakly associated 520
with correctness.521
4 Discussion522
The real challenge is routing, not raw comple- 523
mentarity.The oracle gaps remain much larger 524
than the realized router gains. Even the best router 525
is 8.34 points below the oracle on StrategyQA and 526
12.90 points below it on HotpotQA. The main bot- 527
tleneck is therefore strategy selection, not simply 528
adding search as an alternative decoder.529
Why routing remains hard.The critic router 530
is especially informative. Although it can inspect 531
both candidate traces and should therefore have an 532
advantage over entropy-based routing, its gains are 533
only modest. A likely reason is that CoT traces are 534
often more fluent, while A* traces are more frag- 535
mented or branch-local. As a result, the critic may 536
favor the trace that appears better written rather 537
than the one with stronger reasoning. This points 538
to trace normalization or compression as a natural 539
next step.540
When search is worth paying for.Our qual- 541
itative analysis suggests a fairly clear boundary. 542
Search helps when reasoning requires non-linear 543
evidence aggregation, hypothesis branching, or 544
multi-hop chaining. CoT is often better when the 545
answer follows from direct factual recall or a sin- 546
gle coherent argumentative path. Routing can thus 547
be viewed as predicting the reasoning structure a 548
question requires.549

provides formal support for the search objective 551

5 Related Work558
Chain-of-thought and structured reasoning.559
CoT prompting (Wei et al., 2022) and self-560
consistency (Wang et al., 2023) established the use-561
fulness of explicit intermediate reasoning. Tree-562
of-Thoughts (Yao et al., 2024) and Graph-of-563
Thoughts (Besta et al., 2024) extended this line564
by exploring richer reasoning structures. Our work565
is closest in spirit to Tree-of-Thoughts, but differs566
in using an A*-style priority rule and in explicitly567
analyzing complementarity with standard CoT.568
Search-augmented LLM reasoning.Reasoning569
via planning or search has been explored with570
Monte Carlo Tree Search (Hao et al., 2023), beam571
search (Xie et al., 2024), and other search variants.572
Our contribution in this space is narrower but more573
specific: we define a cost function over natural-574
language reasoning traces, pair it with a conserva-575
tive admissibility result for the heuristic, and study576
the resulting search procedure as a complementary577
strategy rather than as a universal replacement for578
CoT.579
LLM-based evaluation and verification.LLM-580
as-a-judge methods (Zheng et al., 2023) and step-581
level verification approaches such as process super-582
vision (Lightman et al., 2023) motivate our LLM-583
as-critic router. We use a stronger model to ad-584
judicate between traces only when CoT and A*585
disagree, which turns verification into a targeted586
routing problem rather than a full re-reasoning pass587
on every example.588
Reasoning failures and compositionality gaps.589
Prior work has documented systematic reasoning590
failures even in strong language models. Press et591
al. (Press et al., 2023) showed that models often592
fail when individually solvable subproblems must 593
be composed. Huang et al. (Huang et al., 2023) 594
argued that models do not reliably self-correct with- 595
out external feedback. These findings motivate our 596
treatment of CoT and A* as complementary exter- 597
nal strategies rather than expecting a single trace 598
generator to repair itself.599
Routing and adaptive computation.Routing 600
has also been studied at the architectural level, espe- 601
cially in mixture-of-experts systems (Shazeer et al., 602
2017). Our setting is different: we route between 603
reasoning algorithmsat inference time rather than 604
between model components during a forward pass. 605
6 Conclusion606
We benchmarked standard chain-of-thought (CoT) 607
reasoning against a budgeted A*-style search pro- 608
cedure over reasoning traces on StrategyQA, Log- 609
icQA, and HotpotQA, evaluating both standalone 610
performance and their complementarity through or- 611
acle and routing analyses. The main finding is that 612
CoT and A* are strongly complementary: across 613
benchmarks, each method often succeeds where the 614
other fails, yielding large oracle gains and show- 615
ing that non-linear search can add value beyond 616
linear reasoning. At the same time, current routers 617
recover only a limited fraction of this potential, 618
and the strongest practical router remains partly 619
feature-engineered, suggesting that the main chal- 620
lenge is not whether search can help, but when it 621
should be invoked. Overall, our results position 622
A* not as a replacement for CoT, but as a com- 623
plementary reasoning topology that is most useful 624
when linear traces become brittle or prematurely 625
committed. A natural direction for future work 626
is therefore adaptive inference-time strategy selec- 627
tion: designing stronger and more portable routers, 628
integrating CoT and search more tightly within a 629
single reasoning process, and extending the com- 630
parison to larger models, broader benchmarks, and 631

more compute-aware search procedures.632
Limitations633
While our study provides a useful first comparison634
of CoT and budgeted A*-style reasoning, there are635
several natural boundaries to its current scope. Our636
experiments focus on three representative bench-637
marks and one main model family, which already638
support the central complementarity claim, though639
broader evaluation across additional tasks and mod-640
els would further clarify how widely the observed641
patterns extend. In addition, the strongest router642
on two benchmarks uses feature-engineered sig-643
nals, which is informative for analysis but may644
be less portable than lighter-weight entropy-based645
or critic-based alternatives. The search procedure646
itself is budgeted and uses multi-goal voting, so647
the deployed system is best viewed as an A*-style648
approximation rather than exact classical A*. Fi-649
nally, the heuristic relies on self-reported confi-650
dence and entity-coverage signals, which serve as651
practical proxies for prioritizing the search frontier652
but could likely be improved with stronger calibra-653
tion or more faithful relevance estimates.654
